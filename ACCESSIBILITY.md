# Accessibility

Accessibility isn’t a feature I consider after the interesting work is done.
It’s part of the work.

For projects with a user interface or published content, I aim to meet the
[Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2][wcag] at Level AA. That’s a
direction and a standard for the work—not a claim that every project currently
conforms. These repositories span different ages, experiments, and levels of
maintenance. Gaps and regressions can happen.

## Report an accessibility barrier

If something in one of my projects keeps you from using it, please let me know
through [my contact form][contact]. Include as much of the following as you
comfortably can:

- The project and page, screen, or feature involved
- What you were trying to do
- What happened instead
- Your browser, operating system, and assistive technology, if relevant
- Any suggested fix or workaround you’ve found

You don’t need to know which WCAG success criterion applies—or any WCAG jargon
at all. A plain-language description of the barrier is useful.

I maintain these projects in my own time, so I can’t promise a particular
response or repair date. I will take good-faith reports seriously, do my best to
understand their impact, and prioritize fixes as the project’s activity and my
capacity allow.
